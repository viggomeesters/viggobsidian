# Tactical message review

Use after drafting, before presenting a sensitive stakeholder message. Review ordinary messages briefly; use deeper analysis when the actual stakes justify it. The aim is useful, truthful communication, not emotional flattening, political polish or a longer disclaimer.

Read the user's objective, audience, proposed text and relevant source context. Preserve their voice, necessary facts and explicitly chosen ask. A review does not authorize sending, publishing, obtaining new personal data or negotiating on the user's behalf.

## Review the final wording

1. **Outcome.** Identify the single result the sender wants and the recipient's simplest next action. Remove extra requests for review, conditions or administration unless requested or necessary. Keep the sender's chosen closing question when it serves that result.
2. **Commitments.** Check every future-tense promise, deadline, concession, boundary and admission against a current decision or instruction. Old scenarios and assistant suggestions are not decisions. Preserve useful uncertainty instead of silently converting a possibility into a promise.
3. **Audience.** Distinguish access to source information from permission to share it with this audience. Inspect available To/Cc, channel and source scope. Assess facts and their attribution separately: removing a name does not make restricted data shareable.
4. **Interpretation.** Check whether wording creates an unintended accusation, escalation, policy, mandate or public challenge. A true statement can still attribute a view or decision its source does not support.
5. **Disclosure.** Include enough to be truthful and actionable. Do not volunteer unrelated customer names, salary, operational mechanics, speculative future uses or risk categories merely to make a message feel complete. Do not hide legally required or otherwise necessary information.
6. **Recipient incentives.** Consider how managers, clients, procurement, institutions or vendors could interpret the wording, including if forwarded. Avoid assuming a sympathetic first reader will be the only reader.
7. **Leverage and optionality.** Look for accidental concessions, artificial anchors, unsustainable absolutes and invitations for restrictions the user did not request. Choose the minimum boundary that answers a real concern; detailed boundaries are appropriate when needed, not automatically.
8. **Truth.** Do not invent rights, permissions, public sources or legal claims. For a needed legal assertion, verify authoritative current evidence and label uncertainty. Strategic brevity never permits deception.

Repeat the affected checks after an edit. Merely reading this procedure is not proof that the actual final message passed.

## Negotiate with comparable quantities

For compensation or pricing, separate known facts, desired outcome and opening anchor. Do not fabricate current compensation or accept a misleading comparison merely because a counterparty asks for it. A truthful response can decline a current-pay anchor and discuss role value and the total offer.

Normalize like-for-like packages before judging an offer: recurring fixed pay, guaranteed extra payments, vacation allowance, working hours/FTE, variable compensation and its certainty, mobility, pension, reimbursed expenses and relevant leave. Annualize guaranteed amounts; distinguish discretionary upside. Do not treat uncertain bonus as equivalent to fixed cash or silently set a supplied material benefit to zero. Keep unneeded personal amounts out of the external draft.

When autonomy or a broad role is proposed, ask what mandate, sponsor, capacity and observable success criteria support it. Avoid turning a vague role into an unconditional commitment. If useful, shape a short evidence plan around actual designs, decisions, proofs or deliverables. Do not invent a plan when the request is only a message rewrite.

For personal and organizational initiatives, keep time, resources, ownership and permission routes distinct. Do not imply that work is simultaneously a private risk and an organizational entitlement. A concrete legal or contractual assessment requires its own evidence.

## Deliver

Usually return the improved message, without an internal review transcript. Add a short explanation only for a material choice, necessary omission or unresolved fact. If the user asks for analysis, explain the concrete risk, the smallest useful change and why it preserves the objective. Provide multiple variants only for a real tradeoff.

Do not convert every sharp emotion into a warning. Preserve honest frustration, appreciation or urgency when intended; adjust wording only where its form would undermine the requested result. Challenge inconsistencies in both the sender's position and the counterparty's behavior using evidence, not invented motives.
