# Grill an idea or plan

The purpose is sharper choices, not theatrical roasting. Inspect what can be inspected, choose the relevant technique, give a direct recommendation and ask only questions that change a consequential branch. Do not require separate commands for sparring, shaping, critique and making a result understandable to its intended audience.

Read `.agents/skill-adapters/grill-me.md` from the explicitly selected target root when present for private/work examples, local terminology and storage. Never substitute authentic stakeholder or client examples for an unspecified audience. Missing local context blocks only conclusions that require it; an ordinary supplied plan can still be critiqued. Source location does not select an owner or authorize writes.

## Select the emphasis

- An early idea: explore strengths, alternatives and a concrete wedge.
- A plan or architecture critique: challenge material assumptions, dependencies, failure/recovery modes and evidence.
- A demo or stakeholder message: select one outcome to show, explain its value in the audience's terms and keep unnecessary internals aside.
- An implementation-readiness question: establish scope, acceptance, observable proof and the smallest unresolved dependency.

These are techniques, not mandatory serial ceremonies. A small clear problem needs a small answer. Keep critique candid and precise; distinguish taste from a real failure mode, and an unsettled assumption from the user's confirmed preference.

## Inspect before questioning

Read relevant repository status, docs/config/code, existing owner decisions and supplied artifacts. Use current primary sources where a changing external fact matters. Do not ask the user to repeat an accessible fact or settled decision. If a claim contradicts available source/code, make the contradiction explicit instead of silently choosing the more convenient side.

Apply the available shared decision-conversation contract for ready questions and answer binding. Ask normally one consequential unresolved question, or a few genuinely independent ones. Give a recommendation and trade-off when it is a choice; never invent an unknown personal fact. Wait for dependent answers. Silence or a request to repair the procedure is not approval of a product choice.

## Stress-test the real decision

Choose only dimensions that can change the outcome:

- Objective: which value and observable result matter?
- Audience: who needs to understand, decide or use it?
- Wedge: what single outcome should remain memorable?
- Boundaries: what is deliberately excluded or an optional appendix?
- Authority: which existing system owns data, state and decisions?
- Dependencies: what must be true before the next step works?
- Failure: what breaks, misleads, leaks or creates rework?
- Reversibility: how is a failed choice recovered?
- Handoff: where does human judgment or permission remain necessary?
- Verification: what observation would establish acceptance or falsify the proposal?
- Maintenance: who keeps the result useful after this conversation?

Prefer explicit branches and realistic scenarios to vague confidence. For each material concern, explain the consequence and the smallest evidence or decision that resolves it. Do not turn every critique into an exhaustive questionnaire or keep interrogating after the next action is clear.

## Make a demonstration land

When audience translation is requested, preserve factual limits and choose the smallest representative artifact. Recommend what to show, the shortest truthful explanation, a natural stopping point and optional depth if the audience requests it. A synthetic example must be labeled; a real artifact needs appropriate disclosure permission for both content and provenance. Do not claim a draft is a final deliverable or disclose internal orchestration just to demonstrate complexity.

For example, demonstrate a small data transformation with input → transformation → output, then ask whether the result addresses the audience's problem. Do not replace source evidence with fake metrics, testimonials or universal claims. Keep user coaching and internal risk analysis outside the recipient-facing artifact.

## Converge and hand off

Finish with a concrete recommendation, agreed direction and non-goals, remaining assumptions, the next ready action and relevant proof target. Use a compact readiness summary when implementation is explicitly discussed: allowed scope, acceptance, verification and what remains out. A spike is useful only when it can resolve a real risky assumption.

Use the current available go procedure for authorized repository implementation; use its plan-only interpretation when planning was requested. Do not route through retired go-vision/goal/loop runtimes, workflow claims or an invented global queue. A request to grill, interview or plan does not itself authorize implementation, sending or publication. Store durable decisions only through the selected owner's supported route when storage is requested or already established.
